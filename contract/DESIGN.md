# Clear to Pay escrow contract (Concordium testnet)

The rules that decide whether a supplier gets paid run **on-chain**. The AI agent can propose; only the contract can pay, and only when every rule passes. Each decision is logged by the contract itself, so the accountability record cannot be forged by anchoring an arbitrary hash.

## Roles
| Role | Who | Can |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer admin | the buyer's Concordium account | set up signers, agents and suppliers (one `setup` transaction or one at a time; a new supplier is always paid to its own account); create and fund orders naming the counters, the agent, a referee and a latest date; take the money back before the supplier accepts, or after the invoice deadline if the order is not ready to pay and its delivery is not in dispute; ask for a recount; ask for payment; hand the admin role over in two steps |
| Receiver | the buyer's person at the gate | seal and unseal a blind count on orders that name them |
| Approver (optional) | a second buyer person, inside; companies name one, an individual buyer does not | seal and unseal a blind count; after the gate counts, the payment waits for it for the hold period; a different number holds the payment; silence never blocks it for longer |
| Supplier | the supplier's Concordium account | accept an order and name its delivery rep; agree date changes; approve its own invoice by hash; invoice its own confirmed delivery itself if the agent does not; ask for a recount; ask for payment; give the money back; set its own payout account (starts the hold) |
| Supplier's rep | the supplier's driver, named when it accepts (can be the supplier's own account) | seal and unseal a blind count at the gate |
| Referee | a neutral account named on the order, seen by the supplier before it accepts; replaceable only when both sides agree | rule on a disputed delivery: delivered, or refund |
| Agent | the account that owns a registered agent (Agent #1287), named on the order | submit the order's invoice; ask for payment |

Every Concordium account belongs to an identity verified by an identity provider. Signers can also carry a Concordium ID proof (`person_tag`, `id_audit`; see `../docs/F01_SIGNER_IDENTITY.md`); when both counters on an order have one, the contract refuses an order whose two signers are the same person.

## Flow
1. **Order.** The buyer calls `create_order(order_id, supplier, expected_qty, receiver, approver, invoice_by, agent, referee)` and attaches the CCD. The receiver must be a listed signer in that role; the approver is optional (an individual buying for themselves names only a receiver, often their own account) and, if named, must be a different listed signer in that role; the agent must be registered; the receiver and approver must be neither the supplier nor the agent; the referee must be none of the buyer, supplier, counters or agent; the quantity must be above zero. The counters are fixed for this order from now on: removing a signer from the list later does not affect it. The account that funds the order is recorded, and refunds go back to it.
2. **Acceptance.** The supplier calls `accept_order(order_id, invoice_by, rep)` from its own account, confirming the exact date and naming its delivery rep. This is the PO acknowledgment. Before it, the buyer can change the date alone or take the money back. After it, a date change needs both sides (`change_date`): one side proposes, the other confirms the same date within 7 days. A proposal belongs to a side (buyer or supplier), not an account, and its proposer can withdraw it.
3. **Blind counts at the gate.** The receiver and the supplier's rep each call `commit_count(order_id, sha256("CTP-count/v3" ‖ order_id ‖ round ‖ signer account ‖ qty ‖ salt))`. Nobody can unseal until both gate counters have sealed. The seal carries its signer's account and the count round, so a seal copied from another signer, or reused from an earlier round, never opens. Until anyone unseals, a signer can replace their own seal (for example after losing its key). The approver may seal and unseal too.
   **No count, no unload.** The rep's screen shows whether the buyer's receiver has sealed; the rep unloads only after it has.
4. **Delivery confirmed** when the receiver and the rep both unsealed the ordered quantity and the approver, if it unsealed, agrees. Or when the referee rules the goods delivered. If an approver is named and has not unsealed, payment waits the hold period after the receiver's and rep's counts are both unsealed (`HeldApproverWindow`), so a receiver and driver who agree cannot be paid before the approver has counted.
5. **Invoice.** The agent named on the order calls `submit_invoice(order_id, invoice_hash, amount)`. The supplier calls `approve_invoice(order_id, invoice_hash, amount)` from its own account, so an agent cannot get an invented invoice paid, and a buyer who escrows less than the supplier approved cannot pay less. If the agent does not invoice a confirmed delivery (it is offline, or the buyer removed it), the supplier calls `supplier_invoice(order_id, invoice_hash, amount)` itself: only once the delivery is confirmed, only for the full escrowed amount, and it counts as the supplier's approval (`SupplierInvoiced` and `InvoiceApproved` events).
6. **Decision.** The named agent (registered, re-checked in the registry, and holding the same agent token as on the invoice), the supplier, or the buyer calls `execute_payment(order_id, receipt_hash)`. The rules are the same whoever asks. The contract pays only on CLEARED; once paid, the order is final.
7. **Recount.** Once both gate counters have sealed and the delivery is not confirmed, the buyer or the supplier can call `recount(order_id)`: everyone counts again, blind, in a new round. At most 2 recounts; every earlier count stays in the event history.
8. **Dispute.** Once the receiver and the rep have both sealed (the goods are at the gate), the order keeps a record of it (`gate_met`), and a recount does not erase it. If the counts then do not confirm the delivery (a counter disagreed or stayed silent, or a recount was never finished), the referee can `rule(order_id, Delivered | Refund)`. Delivered: the delivery counts as confirmed, and the supplier gets at least 7 days from the ruling to invoice, even after the date. Refund: the money goes back to whoever funded the order. A referee who does not answer can be replaced with `change_referee(order_id, referee)`: one side proposes a neutral account and it changes when the other side proposes the same one (before the supplier accepts, the buyer alone may change it); proposals expire after 7 days.
9. **The latest date.** By `invoice_by`, the delivery must be confirmed. After it the contract takes no new counts. A delivery both gate counters confirmed gets a **grace week** past the date to invoice and approve (by the agent or by the supplier itself), and the buyer cannot take the money back during it. If the order was ready to pay, the supplier is paid. If not, the buyer can take the money back once the invoice deadline has passed, except while a delivery on record (both gate counters sealed) is in dispute: then only the referee can send it back.

## Rules checked by `execute_payment`
Before any rule runs, the call is rejected outright (nothing logged) if: there is no invoice (`NoInvoice`); the caller is not the agent on the invoice (registered, verified, same token), the supplier or the buyer (`NotAgent`); or the order is already paid or refunded (`OrderClosed`).

| Code | Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `HeldOneSignature` | HELD | the receiver's and the supplier rep's counts are not both unsealed (and no referee ruling) |
| `HeldCountMismatch` | HELD | an unsealed count differs from the ordered quantity (and no referee ruling) |
| `HeldApproverWindow` | HELD | a named approver has not unsealed and the hold period since the gate counts has not passed (and no referee ruling) |
| `HeldAmountMismatch` | HELD | the invoice amount is not the escrowed amount |
| `HeldNoSupplierApproval` | HELD | the supplier has not approved this exact invoice hash for this exact amount |
| `HeldPayoutCoolOff` | HELD | the supplier's payout account changed less than the hold ago (72 h in production; init refuses anything outside 1 minute to 30 days, and below 24 hours in a mainnet build) |

On HELD the contract **logs the decision and pays nothing** (the call still succeeds, so the record is kept). On CLEARED it marks the order paid, transfers the escrowed CCD to the supplier's payout account and logs the decision.

## Decision event (the on-chain receipt)
`Decision { order_id, verdict, codes, agent, agent_token_id, receipt_hash, paid_to, at, asked_by }`, emitted by the contract. `receipt_hash` is the SHA-256 of the payment receipt the asker built from the order (see `../receipt/escrowReceipt.js`), so the fingerprint lives inside the very decision that moved, or held, the money.

## Design choices, and what protects the user
- **The AI is never in charge of money.** The agent can submit and ask; the contract checks counts, amounts, approvals and payout age itself.
- **Each side has a counter the other side does not choose.** The buyer names the receiver, the supplier names its rep, and payment needs both counts. The buyer's own counters cannot stall a delivered order into a refund: once both gate counters have sealed, only the referee can send the money back while the counts do not confirm the delivery, and a recount does not change that.
- **A referee for real disputes.** The contract cannot see a warehouse, so when the counts disagree, a neutral referee named on the order (and seen by the supplier before accepting) decides. Every ruling is an event on the record.
- **The approver checks, but cannot stall.** After the gate counts, the payment waits for the approver for the hold period, so it cannot be rushed past them. A different number holds the payment; silence never blocks it for longer than that. The approver is the buyer's own second check, often counting inside after the gate, so it may seal after the gate counts are unsealed. Its power stays narrow either way: agreeing only lets the supplier be paid, and disagreeing sends the delivery to the neutral referee. It can never redirect, reduce or take back the money.
- **A confirmed delivery is the supplier's money.** Only the buyer's agent invoices normally, so the supplier must never depend on it: once both gate counts confirm the delivery, the supplier can invoice it itself, and the buyer cannot refund until a grace week past the date has passed.
- **Only the supplier decides where it is paid.** A new supplier is paid to its own account (`register_supplier` and `setup` refuse any other), and only the supplier can choose another one with `set_payout`, which restarts the hold. A "new bank details" message to the buyer's staff has nothing to change.
- **A silent referee can be replaced, by agreement.** Neither side can swap the referee alone once the supplier has accepted, so neither can pick a friendlier one mid-dispute.
- **Blind counts by commit and reveal**, bound to the signer and the round, so no counter can copy another's number or reuse an old one.
- **Honest mistakes can be fixed.** A lost seal key can be replaced before anyone unseals, and a recount (at most 2, opened by the buyer or supplier, never by a counter) corrects a miscount.
- **Payout changes wait out a hold** (72 hours in production). The hold delays a payment to a new payout account and makes the change visible: a hijacked supplier account cannot redirect a payment that is due now, and the real supplier has the hold period to notice and act.
- **Fail closed.** Anything missing gives HELD, never CLEARED.
- **The contract asks the agent registry itself.** When the buyer adds an agent, and again at every invoice and every payment request by the agent, the escrow calls `agentOf` on the CIS-8004 registry `<12802,0>` and requires the agent to be Active, owned by the calling account and holding the token on the invoice. Anything the escrow cannot read or does not expect is a refusal. The parser is tested against the registry's real answer for Agent #1287.
- **One agent per order.** Only the agent named on an order can put an invoice on it, so another agent cannot jam it. The buyer or supplier can reject a wrong invoice; the buyer cannot reject one the supplier approved for the full amount.
- **One date, agreed by both.** The buyer proposes it, the supplier accepts it, and any change needs both sides within 7 days. After it, nothing new is accepted, so a late invoice cannot sneak in.
- **Nobody waits on the agent.** The supplier or buyer can ask for the decision too; the rules do not change with who asks.
- **Refunds go back to the funder**, even if the buyer admin changes in between.
- **Every change is on the record.** Removing a signer or agent, admin handover, date proposals and changes, referee proposals and changes, recounts and rulings all emit events.
- **The registry address is fixed per contract.** Letting an admin swap it would let that admin point the escrow at a registry it controls. A registry migration is handled by deploying a new escrow version, the same way every version so far has been deployed.
- **CCD first, stablecoin next.** Escrow uses CCD on testnet for the demo; paying out in a PLT stablecoin is the next step.
